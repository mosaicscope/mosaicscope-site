# Lore notes 5: character scene beats

This note gives each of the ten Mosaics a short arc of scene beats. A beat is one moment of the character's story, usually across three or four exchanges. The arcs run alongside the three acts in `02-acts-clues-endings.md` and use the private worries from `01-city-and-cast.md`. They are not quests. Each beat is something the player may happen to hear while spending time with the character, and each one should be complete on its own.

Every arc has the same four beats: an opening (what the character is doing when you first meet them), a crack (the worry shows for a moment), a choice (the character does something about it, or doesn't), and a resolution (the consequence, which may be quiet). The resolutions are deliberately modest. Nobody in this city is rescued by a single conversation.

## Tiles

- **Opening.** Tiles is on the Plaza in the morning, setting up a ball game for nobody. He says he's just warming up.
- **Crack.** Tiles mentions that the crew hasn't all been in one place for a month. He changes the subject to the cones.
- **Choice.** Tiles can throw a party for the crew, or he can go and find them one at a time. He does the party, and it's a little too big.
- **Resolution.** At the end of the party, Tiles sits on the bench from the consultation and watches the empty Plaza. Lux notices and sits next to him without saying anything.

## Glitter

- **Opening.** Glitter is at the market, buying nothing, listening to everyone.
- **Crack.** She admits she is afraid of losing a conversation, and that is why she writes everything in her head. She says it as a joke.
- **Choice.** Glitter can share what she knows about the ledger, or keep it to herself. She tells one person, and makes them promise not to repeat it.
- **Resolution.** The person she told repeats it in the Studio during the rain. Glitter hears it, and nods, and goes back to the market.

## Kodak

- **Opening.** Kodak is always at the edge of a crowd, camera raised, not quite in the shot.
- **Crack.** He mentions a photo he hasn't shown anyone. He doesn't say what's in it.
- **Choice.** Kodak can show the photo, or keep it. He shows it only if the player has already heard the cone pattern from someone else.
- **Resolution.** If the photo is shown, the pattern matches what the busker said. If it isn't, Kodak deletes the photo from his phone, in front of the player, and says it was never worth keeping.

## Nova

- **Opening.** Nova is in the Studio, sitting by the window, watching the rain.
- **Crack.** She says she thinks something is wrong with the city, and she doesn't know what. She says she is too calm to notice.
- **Choice.** Nova can tell the player what she has seen on the bridge at dawn, or wait until she is sure. She waits. She says she will tell them once she is.
- **Resolution.** If the player comes back on a later rainy day, Nova has decided. She tells them about the clerk, and asks them not to tell Tiles, because he will try to fix it with a party.

## Grain

- **Opening.** Grain is at the Stage, setting up a prank for someone who has already guessed it.
- **Crack.** The prank lands badly on a friend. Grain sees the look on their face and goes quiet for the first time in the game.
- **Choice.** Grain can apologise, or double down with a bigger prank. He tries the apology first, and it comes out wrong. He tries the prank again, and it is a smaller one.
- **Resolution.** The friend laughs, a little. Grain takes that as a win, and it's the first honest win he's had in a while.

## Flash

- **Opening.** Flash is at the playground, on the swing, not swinging.
- **Crack.** He mentions a person outside the crew, and he gets angry, then embarrassed, then tells the player not to ask about it.
- **Choice.** The player can ask again, or leave it. If the player asks again, Flash tells a short true story about a fight that happened years ago, and about why he still holds the grudge.
- **Resolution.** Flash says the grudge is not really about the other person. It's about the crew breaking apart once already. He says it quietly, and goes back to not swinging.

## Ruby

- **Opening.** Ruby is in the alley, building a toy from a traffic cone that nobody has reported missing.
- **Crack.** She says she is always counting, and that she knows exactly who owes what. She says it like a joke that has stopped being one.
- **Choice.** Ruby can help the player with a debt, or tell them to pay up. She offers to help, and expects a trade in return.
- **Resolution.** The trade is small and useful. Ruby is satisfied, and says the count is one step closer to being finished.

## Bando

- **Opening.** Bando is on the court, dribbling alone, daring anyone to join him.
- **Crack.** He admits that he picks fights so he doesn't have to talk about being scared. He says he isn't scared. He says it twice.
- **Choice.** Bando can challenge the player to a game, or let them win. He challenges them, and plays properly, and loses on purpose in the last point.
- **Resolution.** Bando says he's never lost to anyone who wasn't trying. He says it with a grin, and it's the most honest thing he's said.

## Lux

- **Opening.** Lux is in the park, learning the name of the busker's dog.
- **Crack.** She says she makes friends so fast that she never has time to keep them. She is not sad about it, only tired.
- **Choice.** Lux can introduce the player to someone new, or ask them to remember one person she has already met. The player can do either.
- **Resolution.** If the player remembers the person, Lux is delighted, and tells the dog. If the player introduces someone new, Lux forgets the first person's name, and laughs at herself.

## Static

- **Opening.** Static is at the Stage, guarding the boombox like it might run away.
- **Crack.** The power flickers. Static panics, quietly, and checks the backup battery twice.
- **Choice.** Static can tell the player about the battery, or hide it. He tells the player, and asks them to keep it secret, because if the crew knew, they would use it all the time.
- **Resolution.** On the next night the power fails, the music keeps going. Static is the only one who knows why, and he doesn't say.

## The Famiglia

These beats belong to the family rather than one member, and they should run in the Don's garage and at Two Pies.

- **Opening.** The garage is quiet. Vito is polishing the Don's car, which has no scratches.
- **Crack.** Gina mentions the ledger has a blank line, and she doesn't know whose name goes there.
- **Choice.** The Don asks the player to deliver a tomato to the casino. The tomato is fragile. The player can take the long way, or the short way.
- **Resolution.** Whichever route the player takes, the tomato arrives. Gina writes it in the ledger, in pen.

## Bryce, Kevin and Quakenstein

These three are separate from the crew and from the Famiglia, so their arcs run on their own.

**Bryce.**
- *Opening:* on the scooter, cape flapping, on the way to a cat.
- *Crack:* the cat is not lost. He is the cat's owner, and he has been looking for it for a week.
- *Choice:* Bryce tells the player he isn't a superhero. Then he takes the signal anyway.
- *Resolution:* the signal goes up. Nobody is sure what it means, but the cat is found.

**Kevin.**
- *Opening:* Kevin is in the park, eating a hat.
- *Crack:* Kevin stops eating and looks at the player for a long moment.
- *Choice:* the player can take the paper out of his mouth, or leave it.
- *Resolution:* if taken, the paper shows numbers that match the ledger. If left, Kevin eats the rest, and the numbers are gone for good.

**Quakenstein.**
- *Opening:* Quakenstein is in the garage, under the Don's car, with a jar.
- *Crack:* He says the jar is not a weapon. He says it again, more quietly.
- *Choice:* the player can help him fix the car, or ask him about the cones. He can only do one at a time.
- *Resolution:* if the player asks about the cones, he says he has no authority over them. If they help with the car, he gives them a small, real demonstration: a single cold patch on the river, for one night.

## Keeping the arcs in balance

- Each character should have at least one beat in each act, so no one drops out of the story.
- Beats should be reachable from the character's home, and also from the places they visit, so the player does not have to chase anyone.
- No two characters should have the same resolution. Some are quiet, some are funny, and one or two should feel unfinished.
- Keep the private worries private until the crack. The player should find them out, not be told.

## Open questions

- Should a character's arc be allowed to end badly, or should every arc resolve at least quietly?
- How many beats does each character need in the first release? The draft says four. Two would be a safer first pass.
- Should the Famiglia's beats be shared with the player through the ledger, or through the garage?
