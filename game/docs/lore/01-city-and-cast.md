# Lore notes 1: the city and the cast

These notes cover the places, the people who live in them, and the small histories that make them feel lived in. The central story is in `docs/LORE_BIBLE.md`. Everything here should be consistent with the names already in `src/content/cast.ts`, `src/dialogue/packs/life.ts` and `src/dialogue/packs/lore.ts`. Where a detail is new, it is marked as new so the writers can check it.

## How the city feels

Mosaicscope is a small city that behaves like a big one. Everyone knows everyone, which means nobody is ever really a stranger, but nobody knows the whole story either. Each neighbourhood has its own gossip, its own opinion about the council, and its own idea of what is normal. The weather decides a lot of the mood. Rain sends people indoors and makes the Studio busy. A clear night brings out the skaters and the busker and makes the Kiwi-signal visible from the hills.

The city is built on a loose grid with a river running through the middle. The Avon is the spine of the place. Bridges cross it at a few points, and the bridges are where most of the stories happen, because that is where people have to stop and talk to each other. The Port Hills sit over the south side and act as the city's backdrop, which is why the signal goes up there.

Time of day matters. Mornings belong to the cafés, the medics and the people going to work. Afternoons belong to the courts, the park and the shops. Evenings are for the casino, the stage and the food stalls. Late nights are the Studio, the alley and the people who don't want to be seen. None of this is enforced, but it is the feel the game should carry, and the lore should match it.

## Places

**The Plaza.** The main square in front of the Cathedral. Tiles' home base. Tourists stop here, buskers play here, and the Council puts up the new benches. Most public announcements are made here, and most of the rumours start here too.

**Cathedral Square.** Next to the Plaza, bigger and older. The Kiwi-signal is best seen from here. The square has a mural of Kevin the giraffe on the wall of a closed shop, painted by someone nobody will name. It is the closest thing the city has to a monument.

**The Avon and the Bridge of Remembrance.** The river runs east to west. The Bridge of Remembrance is the most photographed crossing, and the one the Council wants to protect. Quakenstein's threat is aimed at the river, so every story about the freeze comes back to this bridge.

**The Court.** Where Tiles and the others play ball and where the competitive crowd gathers. Good for rivalries. Glitter likes it because the people watching are the people who talk.

**The Stage.** The music venue and the late-night party spot. Grain stages his nights here, and Bando keeps the boombox going. Nightlife is loud, but the stage is also where people overhear things they shouldn't.

**The Studio.** A quiet creative space. Calm people go here, and it is where people shelter when it rains. Several of the best conversations in the game happen in the Studio, because nobody is in a hurry.

**The Park and the Velvet Zoo.** The park is the everyday green space. The Velvet Zoo sits inside it and is home to Kevin the giraffe, who has opinions about hats. The zookeeper is the person who knows the most about what Kevin has eaten.

**The Alley.** Ruby's place of work: the back streets, the bins, the improvised toys, and the trades. Nothing in the alley is officially for sale. Everything in it is for trade.

**The Playground.** Nova's territory. Quiet, slightly out of the way, and the place people go to hold a grudge. It is where you go to be left alone.

**Two Pies and the casino.** Two Pies is the mince-pie shop where Sal sells the pies that are secretly the Famiglia's interest payments. The casino, with its pokies and its money gun, is the Famiglia's main business. Gina keeps the ledger here.

**Moorhouse and the car yards.** The strip of car dealers and hubcap sellers. It is where the Riccarton Boys do business and where the Pink Pukeko has been for sale for years.

**The Port Hills.** The high ground to the south. The Kiwi-signal goes up here. It is also where the city looks back at itself from a distance, which is why the signal is a shared hope rather than a single person's business.

## The Mosaics

The ten Mosaics are the crew at the heart of the game. Each one has a home, a personality and a way of seeing the city. The notes below give each one a short history and a private worry. None of this needs to be spelled out in the game. Each worry should show up in three or four exchanges, and no more.

**Tiles.** The main guy. Throws the parties and the first ball. His private worry: the crew only stays together because he keeps throwing things. If he stopped, the crew would scatter. He has never said this out loud. He is most himself on the Plaza in the morning, before anyone is awake to need him.

**Glitter.** Tiles' girl and the city's best source of gossip. She knows who owes what, who is seeing whom, and who said something unkind about whom. Her worry is blackouts. She is afraid of losing track of a conversation, and she writes things down in her own way, in her head, so nobody can take them. She and Gina are the two people in town who keep proper records.

**Kodak.** Always has a camera and turns up wherever something is happening. He sees everything and says very little about it. His private worry is that he is only ever a witness, never a participant. He has photos of the cones on the bridge that he has not shown anyone.

**Nova.** The calm one. First to help when someone goes down. Hides in the Studio when it rains. Her worry is that she is too calm to be useful when it counts. She is the person most likely to notice that something is wrong, and most likely to say nothing until she is sure.

**Grain.** The hype man with a short fuse. Starts toy fights and pranks everyone. His worry is that the pranks have stopped being funny to some of the people he loves. He is slowly learning to tell the difference, and the lore should let the player see him learning it.

**Flash.** Quiet. Keeps to the playground and holds a grudge for a very long time. His grudge is with someone outside the crew. Nobody knows what happened. When the player finally learns, it should feel like the city's own memory coming back, not a plot twist.

**Ruby.** The alley tinkerer. Builds improvised toys out of rubbish and trades everything. Her worry is debt. She knows exactly what everyone owes, because she has been counting for years, and she is afraid that one day the count will matter. She is the one character who talks to the Famiglia without fear, because she has always paid them back.

**Bando.** Competitive. Runs the court and will defend a friend from anyone, including Tiles. His worry is that his competitiveness is a way of not being vulnerable. He is the most likely to pick a fight to avoid a conversation.

**Lux.** Curious about everything. Wanders to every crowd, collects shiny things and makes friends quickly. Her worry is that she makes friends so fast she never has time to keep them. She is the person who remembers small details, like the name of the busker's dog.

**Static.** The DJ. Shy until the music is on and guards the boombox fiercely. His worry is the power going out. He has a backup battery he never talks about, and he would rather be ridiculed than have the music stop.

## The people who run the city

**Dr Quakenstein.** A scientist who does not think of himself as a villain. He is proud of a plan to freeze the Avon, and he has a harmless flaw: he is terrible at explaining his plans in a way anyone else can follow, so everyone assumes the worst. He is not sure the cones are necessary, but the Council keeps paying for them, and he has not had the heart to stop the ransom. (New: his reasons for the cones are never fully explained. That is intentional.)

**Bryce.** A self-employed superhero. He files tax returns, worries that the cape is not deductible, and genuinely believes the kiwi-signal is a public service. He takes lost cats seriously. He does not drive a car. He rides a Lime scooter in a cape, which he regards as a responsible choice. (New: Bryce's real name is never used. Nobody in the game has heard it.)

**Kevin the giraffe.** The most famous animal in the city. He has eaten a tourist's hat, a reporter's notes and several pieces of paper with numbers on them. He has no taste and total commitment. He is the only character who knows where some of the numbers went, but he cannot tell anyone, so the player has to work it out from what he has eaten and where.

**The Famiglia.** A small, serious family business with a very large sense of its own importance. The Don is never seen in person in most scenes, only heard about. He cares about his tomatoes, his garage and his respect, in that order. Gina keeps the ledger. Sal sells pies that are secretly interest. Nico is the family's weakest threat and knows it. Vito looks after the Don's car and treats it with the seriousness of a funeral. Mike smuggles things he should not, mostly gnomes and sneakers.

**Gina.** The most dangerous person in the Famiglia, because she is the one who can remember everything. She writes everything in pen. Her ledger is the single source of truth about who owes what. She is not cruel. She is precise, and precision in a debt town looks like cruelty.

**Sal.** The mince pie. Sal's pies are the interest on a debt that started long ago. Nobody remembers the original loan, only that it was large and that the pies keep getting bigger. Sal is the most good-natured person in the Famiglia, which is why people trust him more than they should.

**Terrance.** The voice of the city's live feed. Terrance posts what happens, and the city reads it. Terrance is also the reason some rumours spread fast and some die quickly, so the player should learn to read the feed as carefully as they read people.

**Wilf.** A dry, weary observer of the city's bureaucracy. Wilf's satire is the tone the city uses to laugh at itself. He is useful as a counterweight to the grander stories, because he makes the council's cones look as silly as they are.

## The small histories

**The cones.** The Council started buying road cones after a pothole on the Bridge of Remembrance became a local legend. Nobody can say when the cones started appearing in the wrong places. The first cone was reportedly put on the bridge after a council meeting in which nobody could agree to fix the pothole. The cones have multiplied since. (New: this history is the Council's version and should be presented as rumour.)

**The freeze.** Quakenstein's plan was first mentioned at a science fair, where a small model of the Avon froze in a jar. Nobody took it seriously. The story grew over the years into a threat to the whole river. The truth, which the player should be able to glimpse but never fully confirm, is that the jar was real and the plan was never meant to be a weapon.

**The Pink Pukeko.** A car nobody wants and everyone talks about. It has been for sale for years. The car is the city's running joke about things that never quite sell, and about the people who keep trying to buy them.

**The signal.** Bryce's kiwi-signal goes up over the Port Hills on nights when someone needs help. In practice it goes up when something is wrong, and the people who see it do not always agree on what the problem is. The signal is the city's shared hope, and people argue about it.

**The Riccarton Boys.** A rival group on Moorhouse. They sell hubcaps and make a point of doing so on the Mosaics' turf. The Don takes it personally, which makes them useful as a source of tension rather than a serious threat.

## Notes for the writers

- Keep each character's private worry out of their first line. It should come out in the third or fourth exchange at the earliest.
- Let the city disagree with itself. Two people should be able to tell the same story two different ways.
- Use the weather and the time as cues, not as explanations.
- Never explain the freeze, the cones or the signal in one piece. Each should be a fragment that the player assembles.
- Keep the humour. The city laughs at itself, and the stories land harder when the player can laugh first.
