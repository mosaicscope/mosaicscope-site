# Lore notes 2: acts, clues and endings

This document turns the central story into a sequence the player can actually move through. It builds on `docs/LORE_BIBLE.md` (the threads and the three acts) and `01-city-and-cast.md` (the places and people). The three acts are not chapters the player unlocks. They are layers of understanding. A player can reach the end without seeing every scene, but the ending should feel earned by what they did find.

## The rule of the sequence

Every clue has three parts: the scene where it is heard, the thing it reveals, and the thing it hints at. A clue is never a mystery box. It is a true fact said by someone who does not realise its importance. The player connects facts across conversations and across the day. The engine's job is to make sure no clue is ever locked behind a single place or a single moment.

Two rules protect the sequence.

1. **No clue is required.** Each act has at least two independent paths in. If the player misses a conversation, another one carries the same thread.
2. **No clue is ever wrong.** People can lie, guess or exaggerate, but the game should never tell the player something false as a fact. Rumours are marked as rumours by the speaker, not by the game.

## Act 1: the gossip

Act 1 introduces the city's stories as things people say to each other. Nobody confirms anything. Each thread appears in at least three places so the player hears it more than once before it means anything.

**Scene 1.1: the cones.** On the Bridge of Remembrance, two people talk about the cones. One says the Council is paying a ransom. The other says it is a survey. Both are wrong in different ways. The clue: the cones are arranged in a pattern that changes each morning. Hidden in plain sight: Kodak has a photo of this pattern, but it is only visible if the player has spoken to him about his camera.

**Scene 1.2: the freeze.** A busker on the bridge says the river is going to freeze, and nobody believes him. He has a forecast that is right three days before the weather service's. Clue: the busker knows things before they happen, and so does the city's feed (Terrance), which means the two may share a source.

**Scene 1.3: the signal.** At night, the kiwi-signal goes up over the Port Hills. Two taxi drivers argue about whether Bryce needs a lift. Clue: the signal goes up on nights when the cones have moved. People have noticed this, but they have not said it out loud.

**Scene 1.4: the tab.** Two Pies is busy. Sal jokes about his pies being the interest on a debt. Ruby, at the next table, says she knows exactly what she owes and to whom. Clue: the Famiglia keeps a list, and the list is written in pen.

**Scene 1.5: the zoo.** Kevin the giraffe eats something he should not. The zookeeper says it was paper. Clue: the paper had numbers on it, and Kevin has been eating paper for weeks.

**Scene 1.6: the Pukeko.** Hubcaps are going missing from the car yards, and the Riccarton Boys are selling them on Moorhouse. Clue: the hubcaps come from cars that were parked near the bridge on nights the signal went up. Nobody has connected these yet.

Act 1 ends when the player has heard at least four of the six threads. This is not a gate. It only changes which conversations the game draws from in Act 2.

## Act 2: the pieces

Act 2 is where the threads start to disagree. Characters now have opinions. The cones are a ransom, a prank or a survey, and each person has a reason to believe their version. The player starts to notice that different people know different halves of the same story.

**Scene 2.1: the argument on the Plaza.** Tiles and Glitter argue about whether the cones are a joke. Tiles says it is a Council stunt. Glitter says someone is paying for them, and she has seen the receipts. Clue: Glitter knows who is paying, but will only say that it is "someone with a ledger".

**Scene 2.2: Bryce's denial.** Bryce says the signal is not about the cones. He says it is about a cat. The taxi drivers do not believe him. The player may notice that Bryce is lying, but the reason is not revealed. Clue: Bryce has been to the Velvet Zoo at night.

**Scene 2.3: the Don's garage.** Vito is worried about the Don's car, which has been scratched. The garage is quiet, and Vito says the scratch was made by someone who knows the car well. Clue: the person who scratched the car is someone who has been inside the garage. That person is Quakenstein, who has been fixing the Don's car in return for something.

**Scene 2.4: Kevin's diet.** Kevin eats a whole bag of paper from the Velvet Zoo office. The zookeeper says the paper was the Council's cone survey. Clue: the survey and the ledger are the same paper, which means the Council is keeping its own records of the cones, and they are in the Famiglia's hands.

**Scene 2.5: the Studio.** It rains, and the Studio fills up. Nova, quietly, says she has seen someone walking the bridge at dawn with a box of cones. She will not say who. Clue: the cones are being placed by hand, and the person doing it is working to a schedule.

**Scene 2.6: the Stage.** Grain and Bando argue about who should be allowed to DJ. In the middle of the argument, Static says the boombox power is going to fail at midnight. He is right. Clue: the power failures happen when the signal goes up, which means the signal and the cones may be on the same circuit.

Act 2 ends when the player has at least three of the six scenes above. Again, this is not a gate. It only changes which scenes from Act 3 are available.

## Act 3: who

Act 3 is the answer. The one name missing from Gina's ledger is the person paying the Council's ransom in cones, and that person is the one connecting every thread.

There are two candidates in the draft. The bible says the Don, through Gina. The notes here propose a more interesting answer, which the writers should weigh against the bible:

- **Option A: the Don.** He pays the ransom so the Council keeps the cones on the bridge, which keeps the Famiglia's tab on the bridge's traffic. It is simple and matches the family's power.
- **Option B: nobody.** The ransom is a loop. The Council pays itself through the Famiglia's interest, and the cones are a way of moving money around without anyone admitting it. The missing name is the Council's own clerk, who was never on the ledger at all.

Option B is more in keeping with the city's tone: a town that keeps paying for things nobody needs, because nobody will say stop. It also gives the player a satisfying "so that's what the whole thing was" moment without a single villain to hate.

**Scene 3.1: the missing name.** Gina shows the player the ledger, which has nine pages of names. The tenth name is blank, and the blank line has a date and an amount. The player can match the date to the cones being moved and the amount to the Council's payments.

**Scene 3.2: the bridge at dawn.** Kodak's photos and Nova's report line up. The player, if they have found both clues, can see the person placing the cones: a clerk with a clipboard and a schedule. The clerk does not know why the cones matter. They are paid to place them.

**Scene 3.3: Quakenstein's confession.** Quakenstein, asked directly, says the freeze was never meant to be real. The jar was a demonstration. The Council heard about it and asked for a plan. He wrote one, and it has been used as a threat ever since. He cannot stop the cones, because he has no authority over them.

**Scene 3.4: the signal.** Bryce, pressed, admits that the signal goes up when the Council's payments are late, because the cones start moving and someone has to stop them. He does not know who. He only knows the signal works.

**Scene 3.5: the tab closes.** Sal's pies run out, and the Famiglia has to decide whether to keep collecting the debt. Gina writes the final line of the ledger. The debt is paid in full, and the interest stops. The city is quieter for a day.

## Endings

The endings are not good and bad. They are different ways of leaving the city.

**Ending A: the answer.** The player connects the ledger, the cones and the clerk. The Council is exposed, and the cones stop. The bridge is cleared. The city is slightly poorer and much more honest. Tiles throws a party to celebrate, and it is a little sad.

**Ending B: the loop.** The player finds the answer but chooses not to act. The cones keep moving. The signal keeps going up. The Famiglia keeps the tab. The city gets used to it, and the player becomes one of the people who say nothing. The final line is Gina's: "Pen is permanent."

**Ending C: the freeze.** The player helps Quakenstein turn the jar into a real demonstration. The Avon does not freeze, but a small part of it does, for one night. Everyone goes to the bridge. Bryce says it was a cat. Nobody believes him, and nobody minds.

Endings should be reached through choices the player makes in Act 3, not through a single final decision. Each choice should be visible in the dialogue options and should change which exchanges are available afterwards.

## What the engine needs

- A **clue set** per save: a list of clue IDs the player has heard. Exchanges check the set through a `clue:<id>` tag.
- A **clue memory** for characters: once a character has told the player a clue, they should repeat it only in response to a direct question, not as chatter.
- A **schedule** for the cones and the signal: a small timeline that moves the cones each morning and raises the signal on nights when the Council is late.
- **Endings** as flags set by Act 3 choices, read by the final exchanges.

These are design notes, not code. They should be implemented after the story is agreed. The engine change is covered in `docs/adr/0006-deterministic-dialogue.md`, and the save change in `docs/adr/0007-saves.md`.
