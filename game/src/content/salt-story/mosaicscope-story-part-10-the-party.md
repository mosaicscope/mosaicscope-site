# MosaicScope Story, Part 10: The Party

**Series:** Full conversational storyline, 10 parts. The final part.

**Format:** Scenes with a location, time and cast. Speakers in bold. `[stage directions]` in brackets. Kiwi voice, cartoon tone, no slurs, no real-world politics.

**Picks up from Part 9b:** Tiles' party is on at the stage behind the Market. Everyone in the SALT is invited. Jace has a one-month hubcap trial and Kyle is his assistant. Hemi is bringing a stale biscuit. Grain has said he is over the sandcastle.

---

## Scene 1: The Stage, Saturday Night

*String lights blaze across the stage. The banner reads "NIGHT KIWI: THE RUMOUR IS TRUE." Lanterns line the Market path. The crowd spills out onto the car park: Mosaics, Famiglia, Riccarton Boys, cops, shopkeepers, students, tourists, and a surprising number of ducks at the edge of the pond. Static stands at the boombox, headphones on, nodding. A single bass note rolls out over everyone.*

**Static:** Ok. Music's on. Ok. I'm fine.

**Glitter (from the crowd):** You're more than fine, babe! You're the DJ!

**Static:** Thanks, Glitter. Ok.

*Tiles climbs onto the upturned bucket at the front of the stage, arms spread wide, gold jacket glinting.*

**Tiles:** Ladies, gentlemen, Riccarton Boys, Famiglia, police, surveyors, ducks! Welcome to the party!

*Cheers. A duck quacks.*

**Tiles:** Tonight we celebrate the fact that it hasn't rained since Tuesday, the Cathedral is still standing, and the Night Kiwi is finally a man with a name!

**Hemi (from the crowd, blanket around his shoulders):** Hemi, Tiles. My name's Hemi.

**Tiles:** Hemi! Get up here!

**Hemi:** I'm not getting on the stage, Tiles.

**Tiles:** Everyone gets on the stage, bro. That's the rule.

**Glitter:** Get up there, Hemi! Get my good side!

*Hemi shuffles up onto the stage. The crowd cheers. Hemi raises a hand, shy.*

**Hemi:** Kia ora. I'm a surveyor. I like biscuits. I'm sorry about the ducks.

*Laughter. Someone from the back shouts "The ducks forgive you!"*

**Tiles:** The ducks are very forgiving, Hemi. Ask Mo.

**Mo (from the crowd):** The ducks love me! I'm their poet!

## Scene 2: The Stage, Later

*The party is at full swing. Foam swords clash on the far side of the car park. Flash is trying to juggle three lollies while Ruby runs a court net across the stage. Lux is handing out bottle caps like confetti. Honey stands at a folding table with her clipboard, directing a line of people toward the chilly bin.*

**Honey:** Next! Pineapple or hokey pokey? Don't tell me, I've already got a colour for you.

**Mikey Stone (in a bow tie):** Honey, I'm just here for the shrimp.

**Honey:** You're yellow, Mikey. Eating shrimp, standing guard. That's a dangerous combination.

**Mikey:** I'm not a danger, love. I'm a bouncer.

**Honey:** Bouncers are the most dangerous people at a party, Mikey.

**Mikey:** That's a very fair point, Honey.

*Velvet appears beside the chilly bin, champagne in one hand, Kevin the giraffe's bow tie around his neck.*

**Velvet:** Honey, darling! Do you have anything on the list for me?

**Honey:** You're gold, Velvet. Tiles says you're the most expensive tin of paint in Canterbury.

**Velvet:** That's an honour, darling!

**Honey:** It's not an honour, Velvet. It's a warning.

*Saffron walks up behind Velvet, wearing a sensible jumper for once.*

**Saffron:** Velvet. Stop talking to the clipboard.

**Velvet:** She's very organised, Saffron.

**Saffron:** She's a menace, Velvet. Like you.

**Velvet:** Darling, thank you.

*Kyle and Jace walk up to the chilly bin, a pair of mismatched bare rims rolling along behind them on a rope.*

**Kyle:** Honey, we're here. Jace is the hubcap manager now.

**Honey:** Congratulations, Jace. You're blue.

**Jace:** Blue? I'm not blue, Honey. I'm yellow.

**Honey:** You're blue, Jace. Hubcap manager, on probation.

**Jace:** That's fair, Honey. Can I have a lolly?

**Honey:** It's on the list, Jace. Under "Reformed."

## Scene 3: The Court, Night

*The court behind the stage, lit by a single string of lights. A makeshift net stretches across the middle. Ruby stands on one side, bouncing a ball. Flash stands on the other, holding a foam sword like a tennis racket. Tiles sits on a milk crate, acting as referee, holding a whistle.*

**Tiles:** Right! First to five! Winner gets the lollies!

**Ruby:** Flash, serve or I'm taking your sword.

**Flash:** Ruby, you can't take my sword. It's a foam sword, bro. It's sacred.

**Ruby:** Then serve with it, Flash. Do the thing.

**Flash:** Who wants it?

**Ruby:** I do, Flash. Serve!

*Flash throws the ball. It bounces off the sword, hits the net, flies high over Ruby's head, and lands in the chilly bin.*

**Tiles:** Point to Flash! In the chilly bin!

**Ruby:** That's not a point, Tiles. That's a crime!

**Flash:** It's a very good crime, Ruby.

*Ruby serves. The ball flies at Flash. He ducks and it lands with a thump on the stage.*

**Tiles:** Point to Ruby!

**Ruby:** Thank you, Tiles!

**Flash:** You're cheating, Ruby!

**Ruby:** I'm not cheating, Flash. I'm winning.

*Grain steps out from the crowd, quiet, holding a single lolly. He walks to the edge of the court.*

**Grain:** Can I play?

**Ruby:** Grain? You want to play?

**Grain:** Yeah. I'm over the sandcastle.

**Ruby:** Okay. You're on Flash's side.

**Flash:** Grain, mate, you're on my side!

**Grain:** Nah, Flash. I'm on the lolly side.

*Nova rushes over with a first aid kit and a box of biscuits.*

**Nova:** Grain! You're playing!

**Grain:** Yeah, Nova. Don't cry.

**Nova:** I'm not crying, Grain. I'm happy. It's different.

**Grain:** It's the same, Nova.

**Nova:** Then I'm crying happy tears, Grain. You're allowed to see them.

## Scene 4: The Chilly Bin, Late Night

*The chilly bin is the centre of an odd conversation. Vito sits on an upturned crate beside it, pinstripes unbuttoned at the collar for once, a lolly in one hand and the empty biscuit tin on his knee. Ruth stands opposite him, holding a paper cup of tea. Sal sits at Vito's feet, eating a pav.*

**Vito:** Ruth. You came to my party.

**Ruth:** It's Tiles' party, Vito. You're just in it.

**Vito:** Everything's a party when you're in it, Ruth.

**Ruth:** That's a very Famiglia thing to say, bro.

**Vito:** I'm a very Famiglia man, Ruth. Unfortunately.

**Ruth:** You're not unfortunate, Vito. You're just loud.

**Vito:** Ruth. I owe you.

**Ruth:** You owe me a biscuit tin, Vito.

**Vito:** I have it on my knee, Ruth.

**Ruth:** You've had it on your knee for ten minutes. It's still empty.

**Vito:** Then fill it.

**Ruth:** With what?

**Vito:** With the SALT, Ruth. With people. With lollies and biscuits and gold paint. Whatever you want.

**Ruth:** That's a big tin, Vito.

**Vito:** It's the biggest tin in Canterbury, Ruth.

*Ruth sits down on the crate next to him. They both look out over the crowd.*

**Ruth:** Vito, can I ask you something?

**Vito:** Anything, Ruth.

**Ruth:** Why the protection? Why the lollies? Why the tomatoes?

**Vito:** Because the SALT needed somebody to care, Ruth. And nobody else would.

**Ruth:** The council cares.

**Vito:** The council sends letters, Ruth. I send lollies.

**Ruth:** Lollies aren't caring, Vito. They're sticky.

**Vito:** That's the point, Ruth. Sticky things stay. Like people.

*Ruth laughs. Vito smiles, a rare, soft smile, and lifts the lolly to his mouth.*

**Ruth:** Vito, you're a sentimental old bloke.

**Vito:** I'm a Famiglia, Ruth. We don't do sentiment. We do pineapple.

---

## Scene 5: The Stage, Midnight: The Speeches

*The crowd has gathered in front of the stage. The string lights have dimmed to a soft glow. Hemi stands at the front, blanket around his shoulders, a stale biscuit in one hand. Beside him, Bill sits in a folding chair with his rug, Priya stands with her notebook, and Pip stands with her hands in her pockets. Hobbs is beside her, eating a pie.*

**Tiles:** Right, everyone! Quiet, please! We've got speeches!

**Flash:** Speeches are boring, Tiles!

**Tiles:** Flash, you're boring when you're not talking.

**Hemi:** Can I go first?

**Tiles:** You're the Night Kiwi, bro. You go first.

**Hemi:** Thanks. Okay. I'm not very good at speeches. I've spent a week on roofs and in ponds, and I've spent eleven years on the Cathedral. It's still under renovation. I think it's going to be under renovation forever.

*Laughter from the crowd.*

**Hemi:** But I've learned something. The SALT isn't about the parking, or the tomatoes, or the hubcaps. It's about the people who keep turning up. Even when the lights go out. Even when a duck's in your hat.

**Mo (from the crowd):** Hear, hear, Hemi!

**Hemi:** So thanks, everyone. Here's to the dairy. And to the lights being on.

*He holds up the stale biscuit. The crowd cheers, loud and warm.*

**Bill:** That's the most sensible thing I've heard all year, son.

**Hemi:** Thanks, Bill.

**Bill:** Now, Priya love, you're up.

**Priya:** Me?

**Bill:** You've been writing about us all week. Say something.

*Priya steps to the front, notebook open. She clears her throat.*

**Priya:** Okay. Hi. I'm Priya. I'm a student. I was supposed to write an essay about urban identity. I've been writing about a bench, a bus stop, a dairy, a painting, and a hat. And I've been writing about people.

*She looks up from the notebook.*

**Priya:** The conclusion is that the city isn't the buildings. It isn't the Cathedral, even though it's been under renovation for eleven years. It's the people who keep turning up to the dairy, the bus stop, the stage. Whether they're here for the party, the lollies, or just because the lights are on.

**Ruth (from the dairy door):** That's right, love!

**Priya:** So thanks, everyone. You're my essay.

*Applause. Sal stands, wiping his eyes with a pav napkin.*

**Sal:** Don, it's beautiful.

**Vito:** Sal, sit down. You're crying in public.

**Sal:** I'm crying with love, Don.

**Vito:** Then cry quietly, Sal. You'll ruin the party.

## Scene 6: Dawn, The Duck Pond

*The first light of Sunday. Mist on the pond. The last of the party guests are walking home in twos and threes. Kyle sits on the bench, a fourth hubcap on his lap, shining in the sun. He's holding it up, admiring it.*

**Kyle:** Jace, mate. I got it!

*Jace walks up from the Park path, cap in hand, looking exhausted and pleased.*

**Jace:** You got the last one?

**Kyle:** I got the last one, bro. It was in the duck's nest.

**Jace:** A duck had a hubcap in its nest?

**Kyle:** It was a very determined duck, Jace. It's got a future.

**Jace:** Kyle, that's the best news I've had all week.

**Kyle:** Does that mean I get a raise?

**Jace:** It means you get two lollies, Kyle. Which is a raise.

**Kyle:** Deal.

*Pip walks up the path, Hobbs behind her, carrying two coffees. Hobbs hands one to Jace.*

**Hobbs:** Jace. You owe me a biscuit.

**Jace:** I know, Sarge. I'll bring one Monday.

**Hobbs:** Bring a stale one, mate. Vito likes them.

**Jace:** Sarge, that's the strangest advice I've ever had.

**Hobbs:** It's the SALT, Jace. Everything's strange.

*Pip turns to the pond. She looks at the ducks, then at Kyle with his hubcap.*

**Pip:** Kyle. Is that a Mitre 10 sticker on your hubcap?

**Kyle:** It's a hubcap, officer.

**Pip:** It's got a sticker, Kyle.

**Kyle:** It's gold paint, officer. From the painting.

**Pip:** Kyle, you're going to get me into trouble with Velvet.

**Kyle:** Velvet's already got a painting of a tomato, officer. He doesn't need a hubcap.

**Pip:** Fair enough, Kyle. Put it in the report.

**Kyle:** Under what heading, officer?

**Pip:** "Hubcaps, ducks, and a tomato that's now a hubcap. Probably not important."

---

## Scene 7: The Dairy, Sunday Morning: The Last Scene

*Early sun on the SALT. The dairy neon sign has been switched off for the first time in years, and the lights are on inside. Ruth stands at the counter, tying on her apron. Gurjit is on his ladder, straightening the pizza sign one last time. It sits level, for the first time. Honey sits on a milk crate by the door, writing on her clipboard. Priya stands at the window, notebook closed, looking out at the street.*

**Gurjit:** Ruth! It's straight!

**Ruth:** It's level, Gurjit. For once.

**Gurjit:** It's perfect. Like my pizza.

**Ruth:** Your pizza's still got pineapple on it, bro.

**Gurjit:** That's the perfect part, Ruth.

*The bell above the door rings. Vito walks in, pinstripes immaculate again, followed by Sal. Vito carries the empty biscuit tin under one arm, and a single pineapple lolly in his other hand.*

**Vito:** Ruth. Morning.

**Ruth:** Morning, Vito. You're early.

**Vito:** I'm never early, Ruth. I'm punctual.

**Ruth:** That's a Famiglia word, bro.

**Vito:** It's a dairy word, Ruth. I learned it from you.

*He puts the biscuit tin on the counter, and then, carefully, puts the lolly in it.*

**Vito:** There. The tin's not empty anymore.

**Ruth:** It's got a lolly in it, Vito.

**Vito:** A lolly is a biscuit with ambition, Ruth.

**Ruth:** You're mad, you know that?

**Vito:** It's a statement, Ruth. Please don't ask.

*Ruth laughs. She reaches for the tin and closes the lid.*

**Ruth:** Right. Coffee?

**Vito:** A flat white, Ruth. Two sugars.

**Ruth:** You're a flat white bloke now, Vito?

**Vito:** I'm a bloke with a lolly and a dairy, Ruth. I'm whatever you need me to be.

*Honey looks up from her clipboard, pen poised.*

**Honey:** Final list, everybody. Dairy: open. Pizza sign: level. Parking: free. Ducks: forgiven. Hubcaps: accounted for. Riccarton Boys: reformed, mostly. Famiglia: sticky. Velvet: a tin of paint. Night Kiwi: a surveyor named Hemi.

**Ruth:** Honey, what about the Cathedral?

**Honey:** Still under renovation, Ruth. It's the one thing on the list that's never going to change.

*Priya steps away from the window and walks to the counter. She puts her notebook down, opens it, and writes a single line at the bottom of the last page.*

**Priya:** Ruth, what's the final line of the essay?

**Ruth:** You're asking me, love?

**Priya:** You're the one who told me the lights are the important thing.

**Ruth:** Then write that, love. The lights are on. Somebody's still here.

*Priya writes it down. She closes the notebook and looks up.*

**Priya:** Ruth, can I have a pav?

**Ruth:** You can have a pav, love. But only if you bring your own Roma.

**Priya:** I'll bring a tomato, Ruth. I'll bring a whole crate.

**Ruth:** That's the spirit, bro. Keep the lights on.

*The bell rings again. Outside, the SALT wakes up: a bus groans to a stop, Bill takes his seat on the bench, Mo tunes his guitar, and a yellow hatchback with mismatched hubcaps rolls past, slowly, politely, without a single burnout.*

**Bill (from the bench):** Look at that, Priya. Nobody's doing a burnout.

**Priya:** It's Sunday, Bill.

**Bill:** Give it a minute, love. They'll find a puddle.

*Lights come on across the SALT, one window at a time. The dairy's sign hums. The Cathedral scaffolding catches the sun. The city is open.*

## End of the Story

**The threads, closed:**

- **Ruth and the dairy:** The dairy stays open, with the lights on. Ruth has a deal with Vito, a biscuit tin, and a reputation as the one shop that never closes.
- **Vito and the SALT:** Vito has a truce with Velvet and Ruth. He has a lolly in his biscuit tin, and a new habit of flat whites.
- **Velvet and the painting:** The gold tomato turned out to be Mitre 10 paint. Velvet keeps it on the wall as a joke, and a truce with Vito.
- **The Riccarton Boys:** Reformed, mostly. Jace is the hubcap manager on probation. Kyle is his assistant, with a lolly as a raise. Tanner and Brooke are still working on the tracksuit debate.
- **The Mosaics:** Tiles' party went on till dawn. Grain has forgiven the sandcastle, Flash and Ruby are sharing lollies, and Static's speakers are working again.
- **Hemi, the Night Kiwi:** Unmasked as a council surveyor. He has a stale biscuit, a place at the party, and a new reputation as the rumour who made the SALT talk.
- **Priya:** Her essay is finished. The city, she decided, is the people who keep turning up.
- **Pip and Hobbs:** The report is still going, with headings that get less serious every week.
- **The Cathedral:** Still under renovation. Some things never change.

*The end.*
