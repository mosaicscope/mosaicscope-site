# MosaicScope Story, Part 3: The Morning After

**Series:** Full conversational storyline, 10 parts. Part 1 is "The Cold Snap." Part 2 is "The Nor'wester."

**Format:** Scenes with a location, time and cast. Speakers in bold. `[stage directions]` in brackets. Kiwi voice, cartoon tone, no slurs, no real-world politics.

**Picks up from Part 2:** The power is patchy. Ruth has Vito's lolly bag and a note reading "The Don is watching." The Night Kiwi has warned that the parking fight starts at the dairy. Jace is owed a burnout by Tiles. Pip and Hobbs have lost the hatchback and the tomato is still missing.

---

## Scene 1: Cathedral Square, Morning

*Wet pavement steaming in the sun. Power trucks are parked at odd angles. The Cathedral scaffolding has lost a sheet of tarp, which flaps loose in the breeze. Mrs Tui stands at the front of a small tour group with a clipboard and a fixed smile.*

**Mrs Tui:** Good morning, everyone! Today we see the Cathedral, or what's left of it. Please stay behind the barrier, and please don't climb the scaffolding. The council asks questions.

**Tourist (Dave):** Is the tarp meant to be flapping like that?

**Mrs Tui:** It's a feature, Dave. It's a dynamic renovation.

**Tourist (Dave):** Has it been dynamic for long?

**Mrs Tui:** Eleven years, love.

**Tourist (Dave):** That's a long renovation.

**Mrs Tui:** It's a very thorough council, Dave.

*Mo the busker sits on the steps with his guitar, a laminated permit clipped to the case. He plays three chords and stops.*

**Mo:** Morning, all. Anyone want a song? I've got one about a pigeon.

**Tourist (Dave):** Is the pigeon story long?

**Mo:** It's a very long pigeon, mate. Sixteen verses.

**Tourist (Dave):** I'll pass.

**Mo:** Smart move. The last bloke stayed for all sixteen and then asked for a refund.

*Priya walks past, a laptop under one arm and a coffee in the other. She stops at Mo.*

**Priya:** Mo, can I ask you something?

**Mo:** You can ask. Whether I answer depends on whether you're buying a coffee or a song.

**Priya:** I'm buying the coffee. What do you know about the SALT?

**Mo:** The SALT? Ooh. That's a loud corner, cuz. Everyone's got an opinion, and nobody's got a receipt.

**Priya:** That's the best description I've ever heard.

**Mo:** I'm a poet, love. Also a busker. It's a lot of pressure.

*Hannah comes stomping across the square in snow boots, staring up at the sky, looking like she's been betrayed.*

**Hannah:** Excuse me! Is there any snow? Anywhere? Please?

**Mo:** Not in the square, love. Try the Port Hills.

**Hannah:** I tried the Port Hills! I got a sunburn and a parking ticket!

**Priya:** Did you get the parking ticket from Hobbs?

**Hannah:** No, a little bloke in a dark jacket. He just stuck it on my windscreen and said, "The Don is watching."

**Mo:** Ah. Night Kiwi's been busy. Must be the storm.

**Hannah:** Who's the Night Kiwi?

**Mo:** A rooftop bloke with a very good hat. He's either a hero or a bit of a wally. Nobody knows which.

## Scene 2: The Dairy, Late Morning

*Sunlight streams through the dairy window. The fridges are humming again. Ruth is restocking chips. Gurjit stands in the doorway holding a sign that reads "PIZZA: NO CHANGES." Sal sits on a milk crate, looking small.*

**Gurjit:** Ruth, I've decided. I'm not taking the sign down.

**Ruth:** Good for you, bro. The council can come and yell at me.

**Gurjit:** They already did. Yesterday. They said the sign's a hazard to the Cathedral.

**Ruth:** The Cathedral can take a sign, Gurjit. It's been under scaffolding for eleven years.

**Gurjit:** That's what I said! They said it's a different kind of hazard.

**Ruth:** What kind?

**Gurjit:** The kind that makes people hungry.

**Ruth:** That's not a hazard, bro. That's a marketing strategy.

*Sal clears his throat.*

**Sal:** Ruth. The Don has a message.

**Ruth:** Sal, you've been in here three times this week. You're not a message service.

**Sal:** I am today. The Don's taken a new interest in you.

**Ruth:** Oh, I'm so honoured.

**Sal:** He wants a meeting. Somewhere neutral. He says the Riccarton Boys have been messing with your pavement.

**Ruth:** They have, Sal. They've parked a yellow hatchback across my bins. And there's a burnout mark outside my door that looks like a tomato.

**Sal:** That's the Don's signature, Ruth. He's very precise about tomatoes.

**Ruth:** Sal, the Don is not my problem. The Riccarton Boys are my problem.

**Sal:** Then the Don will take care of them.

**Ruth:** For a fee.

**Sal:** For lollies. The Don likes lollies.

**Gurjit:** Everyone likes lollies, Sal. That's not a business model.

*The bell rings. Jace walks in with a broom over his shoulder, wearing a Riccarton Motors cap pulled low. He looks at Sal, then at Ruth, then at the chips.*

**Jace:** Morning, Ruth. Morning, Sal. Morning, pizza.

**Gurjit:** It's a sign, Jace. Not a pizza.

**Jace:** It's a pizza-shaped sign, bro. I can tell.

**Ruth:** What are you doing with a broom?

**Jace:** Cleaning up. Burnout residue, Ruth. Happens when the Boys get excited.

**Ruth:** Your burnout marks are on my pavement, Jace.

**Jace:** It's a community art project. We're making a statement about parking.

**Sal:** The statement is that you owe the Don a tomato.

**Jace:** Sal, I'm not paying for a tomato. I'm paying for a parking space.

**Sal:** The SALT parking's not for sale.

**Jace:** Everything's for sale, Sal. Even your nonna's pasta sauce.

*Sal stands up very slowly. He puts his hands in his pockets.*

**Sal:** Don't talk about my nonna, Jace.

**Jace:** Ease off, Sal. I'm just being honest. Honesty's a sign of respect.

**Ruth:** Jace. Sal. Out. Both of you.

*Jace lifts his hands in mock surrender and backs toward the door. Sal doesn't move.*

**Ruth:** Sal. I said out.

**Sal:** I'm not leaving till I get an answer.

**Ruth:** The answer is I'll meet the Don at the Market, Sal. Tell him to bring his own lollies.

**Sal:** That's not an answer, Ruth. That's a dare.

**Ruth:** Yeah, well. I'm a dairy owner, bro. I deal in dares and milk.

## Scene 3: Riccarton Road, Mid-Morning

*A row of car dealerships, polished cars, lots of plastic flags. The Riccarton Motors sign flickers. Kyle is under the bonnet of a beaten-up ute. Tanner and Brooke lean on the wall, eating hokey pokey ice cream from the same cone.*

**Kyle:** Right, you two. Stop sharing that cone, it's gross.

**Tanner:** It's a cone, Kyle. It's meant to be shared.

**Kyle:** It's a single cone for two blokes, bro. That's a disease waiting to happen.

**Brooke:** It's a bonding experience, Kyle.

**Kyle:** It's a bad idea, Brooke. Look at your face. You've got hokey pokey on your nose.

**Brooke:** That's my signature, Kyle.

**Kyle:** Your signature's a bit runny, mate.

*Jace comes around the corner, cap still pulled low. He sees Tanner and Brooke and beckons them over.*

**Jace:** Oi. Lads. Got a job for you.

**Tanner:** Job? I'm on lunch, bro.

**Jace:** It's a lunch job, Tanner. A proper one.

**Brooke:** What's the job, Jace?

**Jace:** We've got to get Pip off our tail. She's been following us since the party.

**Tanner:** Pip's a bit keen, bro. She's keen on burnouts.

**Jace:** Yeah, and she's keen on the hatchback, Tanner. So we're going to give her something else to be keen on.

**Brooke:** What's that?

**Jace:** A decoy. A mate's car, parked near the Market, with the lights on and the bass up.

**Tanner:** Whose car?

**Jace:** Kyle's ute.

**Kyle (from under the bonnet):** Oi! I heard that!

**Jace:** It's a loan, Kyle. You'll get it back.

**Kyle:** I'll get it back when the bonnet's fixed, Jace. Which will be never.

**Jace:** Good on you, Kyle. Now go and make it loud.

*Kyle gets up from the bonnet, wiping his hands on a rag. He looks at the ute, then at Jace.*

**Kyle:** Jace, mate, that ute's been sounding like a sad goose for a week. If I make it loud, it'll fall apart.

**Jace:** That's the point, Kyle. Let the goose out.

**Kyle:** You're mental, you know that?

**Jace:** Yeah, but I'm handsome.

**Brooke:** That's debatable, Jace.

**Jace:** Don't make me give you a burnout, Brooke.

**Brooke:** You'd never, bro. You love me too much.

## Scene 4: The Market, Lunchtime

*The Market is buzzing again after the storm. Stalls are open, pav is selling fast, and someone has set up a tiny stage with an upturned milk crate. Glitter, Ruby and Flash are sitting on the edge of the stage eating pav with their fingers.*

**Glitter:** Okay, girls and boys. I've got the gossip, and it's sticky.

**Ruby:** Glitter, it's always sticky.

**Glitter:** This is extra sticky. The Famiglia are selling the SALT parking to the Riccarton Boys.

**Flash:** No way.

**Glitter:** Yes way. Jace has been meeting Sal at the casino kitchen. Tony saw them.

**Ruby:** Tony sees everything. He's got the eyes of a hawk and the memory of a goldfish.

**Glitter:** He saw the money, Ruby. And he saw the tomato. There's a tomato involved.

**Flash:** There's always a tomato.

**Glitter:** This is different. This is a Roma. Vito's favourite. The one he won't let anyone touch.

**Ruby:** Vito's a bit precious about his tomatoes, Glitter.

**Glitter:** Precious, yeah. But he gave it to Jace, Ruby. As a sign of goodwill.

**Flash:** Goodwill? Vito doesn't do goodwill. He does lollies and threats.

**Glitter:** Then maybe the threats are the goodwill.

*Tiles walks up with a plate of pav balanced on his palm like a waiter. He's got Honey trailing after him, clipboard in hand.*

**Tiles:** Who's ready for a parking war?

**Glitter:** Tiles! You've been talking to Jace!

**Tiles:** I've been talking to Jace for ten minutes, Glitter. He's a bit of a pest.

**Honey:** He's on the list, Tiles. Red zone.

**Tiles:** Honey, I don't need a red zone. I need a plan.

**Honey:** I've got a plan. It's colour-coded.

**Ruby:** Honey, your plans are always colour-coded.

**Honey:** That's because I'm organised, cuz. You should try it. You might survive a Tuesday.

**Tiles:** Okay, okay. Here's the plan. We go to the dairy. We stand next to Ruth. We make sure nobody takes her bins.

**Flash:** That's it? That's the whole plan?

**Tiles:** It's a good plan, bro. It's a dairy-protection plan.

**Honey:** It's a protection plan with a bin strategy.

**Flash:** I love a bin strategy.

**Ruby:** You love anything that involves bins, Flash.

**Flash:** That's not true. I love the swords too.

*Lux bounces up on her scooter, nearly crashing into the pav stall. She slides to a stop, holding a bottle cap.*

**Lux:** Found another one! It's blue!

**Glitter:** Lux, you're a menace.

**Lux:** I'm a treasure hunter, Glitter. There's a difference.

**Tiles:** Lux, where did you find it?

**Lux:** On Riccarton Road, behind the dealership. There was a yellow car there, and the bloke said I could have it if I helped him fix his hubcap.

**Tiles:** Did you help?

**Lux:** I helped him hold the bolt. He said it was a bad bolt.

**Tiles:** Lux. Don't help Riccarton Boys with their hubcaps. Seriously.

**Lux:** But they're shiny, Tiles! Look!

## Scene 5: The Velvet Mansion, Afternoon

*Velvet Mansion is all gold leaf and velvet ropes. Mikey Stone stands at the front door like a fortress. Saffron lounges on a chaise longue, scrolling her phone. Kevin the giraffe is tied to a pillar, looking bored and slightly guilty.*

**Mikey:** Name?

**Priya:** I'm not here for the gala. I'm here for an interview. For my essay.

**Mikey:** Not on the list, love. Maybe next time. Or the time after.

**Priya:** It's for my uni assignment. I'm writing about urban identity.

**Mikey:** Urban identity's a bit much for a bloke at the door. Come back with a tie.

**Priya:** I don't have a tie.

**Mikey:** Then borrow one from Kevin. He's got a lovely collection.

*Kevin sneezes. A small, cheeky shower of confetti falls from the pillar.*

**Saffron:** Oh. You. Did you bring my shoes? No. Course you didn't.

**Priya:** Sorry. I don't know you.

**Saffron:** Nobody does, love. That's the glamour.

**Priya:** Are you a model?

**Saffron:** I'm a lot of things, babe. Mostly I'm bored. The gala's tonight and nobody's brought anything interesting.

**Priya:** What's the gala for?

**Saffron:** Velvet's bought a painting. A fancy one. He says it's a tomato.

**Priya:** A tomato?

**Saffron:** A Roma. In gold. He thinks Vito will be very jealous.

**Priya:** Oh my god. That's the story, isn't it? Vito's tomatoes.

**Saffron:** It's a story, love. Whether it's a good one depends on who's holding the knife.

*Sir Ponsonby Velvet appears at the top of a sweeping staircase, wearing a silk dressing gown and a lot of confidence. He surveys the room.*

**Velvet:** Ah! Who's this? A visitor! Darling, come up, come up.

**Priya:** Hi. I'm Priya. I'm doing a university essay.

**Velvet:** How marvellous. Do you know the tomato?

**Priya:** The Roma?

**Velvet:** The Roma. The one the Don loves. The one he won't let anyone touch.

**Priya:** I've heard about it.

**Velvet:** Well. It's coming here tonight. I've bought it.

**Priya:** You've bought Vito's tomato?

**Velvet:** I've bought the tomato's *picture*, darling. The Don doesn't know yet. It's a gift, you see. A tribute. He'll be so moved.

**Priya:** I don't think he'll be moved. I think he'll be furious.

**Velvet:** Yes, darling. That's what makes it fun.

*Priya scribbles frantically in a notebook.*

**Velvet:** You'll come tonight, of course. The gala. Wear something shiny.

**Priya:** I haven't got anything shiny.

**Velvet:** Then borrow Kevin's bow tie. He won't mind. He's a giraffe. He doesn't care about fashion.

**Kevin (a noise like a sad goose):** *(Honk.)*

**Velvet:** See? He agrees.

## Scene 6: Night Kiwi's Roof, Dusk

*The sun sets over the SALT. Lights come on in the city. A thin layer of cloud has moved in. Night Kiwi sits on the edge of a rooftop, legs dangling, a pair of binoculars in one hand and a half-eaten pie in the other.*

*Pip climbs up the fire escape, out of breath, holding a torch.*

**Pip:** Oi! You're the Night Kiwi, aren't you?

**Night Kiwi:** I'm not here, officer. I'm a rumour.

**Pip:** Rumours don't eat pies.

**Night Kiwi:** This one does. It's a very determined rumour.

**Pip:** I've been looking for you for three days.

**Night Kiwi:** Then you've been looking in the wrong place. You should look up.

**Pip:** I'm looking up, bro. You're on a roof.

**Night Kiwi:** Exactly. You're getting better.

**Pip:** Why are you watching the dairy?

**Night Kiwi:** Because it's about to be the most interesting place in the SALT, officer. Ruth's going to be in a meeting tonight with the Don. And the Riccarton Boys are going to make sure it doesn't go well.

**Pip:** Why would they do that?

**Night Kiwi:** Because the Famiglia have the lollies and the Riccarton Boys have the parking. And nobody's giving up either.

**Pip:** And what are you going to do?

**Night Kiwi:** I'm going to watch, and then I'm going to decide who's the worst.

**Pip:** Can I help?

**Night Kiwi:** You can help by not writing a ticket for the kiwi mask.

**Pip:** It's a mask, not a bird. You can't wear a bird.

**Night Kiwi:** It's a statement, officer. Please don't ask about it.

*Pip sits down next to him on the edge of the roof, looking out over the city.*

**Pip:** It's a nice view.

**Night Kiwi:** It's the best view. You can see the Cathedral, the Market, and every bloke who parks on the footpath.

**Pip:** Bill says the same thing.

**Night Kiwi:** Bill's a wise man. He just forgot to mention the pies.

**Pip:** Do you ever feel like nobody's on the same side?

**Night Kiwi:** All the time, officer. That's why I wear the mask. So nobody knows which side I'm on.

**Pip:** I think they know, bro.

**Night Kiwi:** Then they're smarter than I thought.

*A light flickers below, in the SALT. Both of them look down.*

**Pip:** Is that the dairy?

**Night Kiwi:** That's the dairy. It's starting.
